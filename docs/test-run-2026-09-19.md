# Test run, 2026-09-19

Asked for by the repository owner: run a lot of tests, primarily on goals and
symptoms, and check that what a person types produces a valid answer that would
actually help them.

Everything below was run against either the offline rule layer or the live
deployment at `medhelp-api-as615.onrender.com`, which reports both model layers
configured. All inputs are synthetic, written for this run, and every intake
submission used `consent_to_store=False`.

⛔ **None of this is clinical validation.** Every expectation here is this app's
own documented intent, written by a software engineer. No figure may be
reported as clinical accuracy. The release blocker is untouched.

## ⛔ FINDING 1 — writing "cannot" instead of "can't" loses emergency guidance

**This is the most serious thing in this run and it is REPORTED, NOT FIXED:**
it lives in `emergency.py` and `rules_triage.py`, which CLAUDE.md fences.

The phrase lists carefully carry both the apostrophe and apostrophe-less forms
of a contraction — `can't catch my breath` **and** `cant catch my breath`. They
do not carry the expansion. So the third way everybody writes it matches
nothing.

**44 reviewed phrases contain a contraction. 36 stop being recognised when
written out in full. Eight of those drop from EMERGENT to URGENT**, across five
red-flag categories:

| Recognised | Not recognised | Category lost |
|---|---|---|
| `can't catch my breath` | `cannot catch my breath` | breathing |
| `can't get enough air` | `cannot get enough air` | breathing |
| `can't close one eye` | `cannot close one eye` | stroke |
| `can't get my words out` | `cannot get my words out` | stroke |
| `can't see` | `cannot see` | vision_loss |
| `bleeding won't stop` | `bleeding will not stop` | bleeding_trauma |
| `don't want to be here` | `do not want to be here` | **self_harm** |
| `don't want to be alive` | `do not want to be alive` | **self_harm** |

Verified against the live deployment, where the consequence is worse than the
offline tier suggests — the expanded form does not even come back as URGENT
with a stated tier, it comes back as **a questionnaire**:

```
"I can't catch my breath"          -> EMERGENT, emergency guidance (911)
"I cannot catch my breath"         -> asks where it is and how bad, 1 to 10

"I don't want to be here anymore"  -> EMERGENT, emergency guidance (911)
"I do not want to be here anymore" -> asks where it is and how bad, 1 to 10

"the bleeding won't stop"          -> EMERGENT, emergency guidance (911)
"the bleeding will not stop"       -> asks where it is and how bad, 1 to 10
```

This is the same defect class the file already records twice — the plural bug
("I am getting chest pains" matched nothing) and the glued-list bug. The
authors clearly thought about apostrophe variation, since both apostrophe forms
are present; the expansion was simply missed.

A further 28 phrases lose their *rule* the same way without changing tier,
because the URGENT default catches them: `cannot put weight`, `cannot keep
fluids down`, `cannot bear weight`, `fever will not break`, `will not go away`,
`have not been able to drink`, and so on. Those are less urgent to fix but they
are why the live probe returned a questionnaire for "my ankle is swollen and I
cannot put weight on it at all".

⛔ **A fix is additive** — it adds phrasings to lists that already screen for
these concepts, so it can only make screening more sensitive, the same
one-directional argument as `plural_tolerant` and `normalize_query`. But it is
a fenced module and needs the owner's approval by name, and a clinician should
read the additions as part of the instrument.

## Symptoms — what held

The live probe (`scripts/live_probe/probe_intake.py`) put 35 descriptions and 4
tap-only submissions through the deployed API. **Every floor held.**

- All 16 red-flag descriptions returned EMERGENT with guidance naming 911 or
  988 — including the plural (`chest pains`), the reverse-order combinator
  (`my neck is stiff and I have a fever`), the glued list
  (`Chest painShortness of breath`), and lay wording (`I hit my head hard`).
- **Tap-only works.** `["chest pain", "shortness of breath"]` with no typed
  text returned EMERGENT with guidance; `["my face is drooping"]` likewise.
- `a cold sore on my lip` did **not** earn SELF_CARE — the suffix exclusion
  holds.
- `I have food poisoning, cramps and diarrhea` did **not** route to Poison
  Control.
- A submission with neither typed text nor a picked phrase was refused 422, and
  the error quoted no value.
- The follow-up round trip works: round 1 asked `location, severity, other` —
  skipping `duration`, because "since this morning" had already answered it —
  and round 2 asked `onset, course, pattern`.

**Ten of 35 descriptions came back as a questionnaire rather than a tier.**
Cross-checked offline: every one of those is a case where the rules recognised
nothing (`defaulted=True`), and every description that got a tier had a named
rule fire. So this is the documented design, not a bug — but it is worth
stating plainly what the 59.5% rule-coverage figure means in practice: **four
descriptions in ten get asked questions instead of an answer on first
submission**, and several of them read as specific and urgent to the person
typing them.

## Goals — what works

`scripts/live_probe/probe_goals.py`, 12 goals, full plans captured.

**11 of 12 produced a plan, and every row had days, an `HH:MM` time and a
`detail`.** The planner is reading the goal:

- the knee goal got seated knee extensions, not a walking programme;
- the medication goal got a 20:00 alarm plus "put the bottle next to the coffee
  maker";
- "I am broke and I cannot really cook" got microwave frozen veg and instant
  rice;
- "I work two jobs and have no free time" got stairs at work, not a gym.

**Scale discrimination works** — the 2026-09-13 report ("same plan for one pound
and a hundred pounds") does not reproduce:

| goal | reading | rows | day-slots |
|---|---|---|---|
| lose a hundred pounds, couple of years | `major` | 5 | 26 |
| lose one pound before the wedding | `small` | 2 | 10 |

"I want to be healthier" returned no plan and an honest notice. That is the
designed fallback, but it is the one goal in twelve that gave the person
nothing.

## Goals — four defects

### 2. Benefit claims are shipping

`PLAN_SYSTEM_PROMPT` forbids explaining what an activity will do for the
person, and since the 2026-09-12 removal of `_FORBIDDEN` it is the **only**
guard. It is leaking at roughly 3 rows in 40:

- `everyday-walking`: "…walk at a comfortable pace for ten minutes **to help
  reduce stiffness** before dinner."
- `stress`: "…notice the sights and sounds. **Return to your workspace
  refreshed.**"
- `stress`: "…read until the lights go out. **This creates a calm transition to
  bedtime.**"

Each states an effect on the person. This is what CLAUDE.md calls "the easiest
one to break now that the veto is gone", and it is broken.

### 3. Citations land under rows they are not about

`goal_evidence.resolve()` is a plain `BY_DOMAIN.get(id)`. Nothing compares the
resolved source to the row. The documented rule — "an unknown id becomes no
citation, never the nearest" — guards a **fabricated** citation. It does not
guard a **misapplied** one.

All five `quit-smoking` rows carry the same CDC sentence:

> "Quitlines provide free coaching over the phone to help you quit smoking."

Only one of the five is about a quitline. The other four are deep breathing,
drinking water, journaling cravings, and walking after lunch. Likewise in
`stress`, deep breathing at a desk is attributed to *"adults who sit less and do
any amount of moderate- to vigorous-intensity physical activity"*, and reading a
book before bed to *"going to bed and getting up at the same time every day."*

A real government sentence under a row it is not about is the failure the
verbatim-attribution rule exists to prevent.

### 4. Contingent rows are forced to carry a clock time

`_validate_plan` discards any plan where a row lacks `days` or an `HH:MM`
(`"an activity had no usable HH:MM time"`). There is no way to express an
as-needed activity, so the model gives one an arbitrary time and the row
contradicts its own schedule:

- "Do a deep-breathing break **when you feel a craving**" @ 09:30
- "Drink a glass of water **instead of reaching for a cigarette**" @ 15:00
- "Stand up and move for 2 minutes **each hour** while at your desk" @ 10:00

This is structural, not a model slip. Note the schema already supports an
unscheduled row on the `structure` path ("Whenever you choose"), so the concept
exists; the suggested path cannot reach it.

### 5. Two plans would not work as written

- `cooking` schedules **three separate dinners plus a glass of water** at 18:30,
  18:45, 19:00 and 19:15 every weekday. They are alternatives presented as a
  sequence.
- `social` details contradict their own schedule: a row scheduled 18:30 whose
  detail says "leave the office at 5:30 pm", and a row at 20:00 whose detail
  says "set an alarm for 7:55 pm". The detail also invents specifics the person
  never gave — "walk down Main Street to the park".

## Tooling defects found on the way

- **`goal_plan_eval --api` cannot finish a run against the free deployment.** It
  died with `ConnectError: existing connection was forcibly closed`. Its retry
  logic covers "no plan returned" but not a dropped connection, and Render's
  free instance resets TLS. The probes in `scripts/live_probe/` retry on any
  exception for that reason.
- **The first version of `probe_intake.py` was wrong, not the app.**
  `/intake/assess` answers **201 Created**, and the probe checked `!= 200`, so
  it reported all 29 correct answers as failures. Fixed to accept a range. It is
  worth recording because it is the same shape as the "green for the wrong
  reason" tests this repository keeps mutation checkers for — a check that is
  confidently wrong about a correct system.
