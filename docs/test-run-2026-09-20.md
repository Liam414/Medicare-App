## ⛔⛔ FINDING 0 — two descriptions are told they will settle on their own

**The most serious thing in this run.** Everything else here is a missed
escalation: URGENT instead of EMERGENT, "get seen soon" instead of "call an
ambulance". These two are different in kind. They come back **SELF_CARE**.

| description | tier today |
|---|---|
| "a dry cough and I cannot catch my breath" | **SELF_CARE** |
| "sore throat and I am drooling and cannot swallow" | **SELF_CARE** |

`rules_triage.py` calls the rule these break the single most important one in
the module: *SELF_CARE must be positively earned... not recognising something
is not the same as it being harmless.* Here recognition of the wrong half is
what does the damage — a self-limiting phrase matches positively while the red
flag beside it does not.

### The first one is a spelling bug, and the app already disagrees with itself

    "a dry cough and I can't catch my breath"   -> EMERGENT
    "a dry cough and I cannot catch my breath"  -> SELF_CARE

This is **not a new clinical claim**. The app's own reviewed phrase lists say
this description is EMERGENT; writing "cannot" instead of "can't" defeats them.

⛔ **It also makes the 2026-09-19 contraction finding worse than that run
recorded.** FINDING 1 of `test-run-2026-09-19.md` measured contractions written
out in full and reported 8 phrases dropping **EMERGENT → URGENT**. In isolation
that is right — "I cannot catch my breath" alone returns URGENT. But put the
same expansion next to a self-care phrase and three things line up:

1. the red flag does not fire, because the list has the contraction only;
2. `a dry cough` matches `self_limiting_complaint` **positively**;
3. `cannot catch my breath` is not in `_ESCALATING_MODIFIERS`, so nothing
   takes the self-care match back.

The result is EMERGENT → **SELF_CARE**. The documented consequence of that
finding understates its worst case, and the two findings should be read
together.

### The second is a gap in the escalating modifiers

`sore throat` earns self-care, and neither "drooling" nor "cannot swallow" is
an escalating modifier. Whether drooling with an inability to swallow deserves
its own red-flag category is a clinician's call this run does not make — but
that it is not self-care is not in doubt, which is why it is pinned with a gold
of URGENT rather than EMERGENT.

### ⛔ It is not two cases. It is 61% of FINDING 1, and it corrects this report

Crossing the 41 phrasings FINDING 1 shows are missed with five ordinary
self-care openers ("a dry cough and…", "a sore throat and…", "a head cold
and…") gives 280 combinations. **170 of them — 60.7% — return SELF_CARE from
the rule layer.**

    a dry cough and my lips are turning blue          -> SELF_CARE
    a sore throat and half my face has gone slack     -> SELF_CARE
    a head cold and my chest is being crushed         -> SELF_CARE

⛔ **An earlier section of this document said "a miss is not a reassurance —
the rule layer defaults to URGENT". That is only true when the sentence
contains nothing else.** People list symptoms together, and the moment a missed
red flag shares a sentence with a recognised minor complaint, the self-care
phrase matches positively and carries the whole description. The correction
matters more than the original claim: it is the difference between "this
under-triages" and "this reassures".

These are not 170 defects. They are one defect — FINDING 1's phrasing gap —
meeting the rule that self-care matches positively. Fixing FINDING 1 closes
almost all of them.

### What the model layer does and does not rescue

Checked against the live deployment, which has a model configured:

| description | rule layer | live |
|---|---|---|
| "a dry cough and my lips are turning blue" | SELF_CARE | **EMERGENT** |
| "a sore throat and half my face has gone slack" | SELF_CARE | **SELF_CARE** |
| "a dry cough and I cannot catch my breath" | SELF_CARE | **SELF_CARE** |

The first is the two-layer design working exactly as documented — the rules
said SELF_CARE, the model said EMERGENT, `max()` took EMERGENT. Credit where
due. But it rescued one of three, and the two it missed include a described
stroke.

Two things follow, and both matter:

- ⛔ **A deployment with no model key runs on rules alone**, and the model layer
  is documented as optional ("a missing key degrades quality, it does not break
  the feature"). On such a deployment every one of the 170 is reassurance.
  The rule layer is described in CLAUDE.md as *the product*; the model is *an
  optional upgrade*. This finding is in the product.
- **A model escalation carries no emergency *payload*, but the screen covers
  it.** On the case it rescued, the response was tier EMERGENT with
  `emergency: null`, because that copy comes from `screen_for_emergency`,
  which never matched.

  ⛔ **An earlier draft of this document called that a second defect. It is
  not, and the correction belongs on the record.** `IntakeResultScreen`'s
  EMERGENT branch renders `EmergencyCallBar` and falls back to "Get emergency
  care now." / "…Call 911 or your local emergency number, or go to an
  emergency department now.", inside an `accessibilityRole="alert"` live
  region. So a model-only escalation still reaches the person as an emergency
  with a number to ring. The client was already built for exactly this case.

## ⛔ FINDING 1 — 73% of ordinary ways of describing an emergency are missed

**The most serious thing in this run, and it is REPORTED, NOT FIXED:** every
line lives in `_EMERGENCY_RULES` in `emergency.py`, which CLAUDE.md fences.

`scripts/triage_eval/phrasing_sweep.py` asks what the two corpora cannot: does
an existing category recognise ordinary ways of describing it? 56 lay phrasings,
each of a presentation the app **already holds a category and reviewed copy
for**. 41 are missed.

| category | recognised |
|---|---|
| stroke | **0 / 5** |
| bleeding_trauma | **0 / 6** |
| vision_loss | **0 / 4** |
| overdose_poisoning | 1 / 5 |
| breathing | 1 / 6 |
| cardiac | 2 / 8 |
| anaphylaxis | 1 / 4 |
| consciousness | 2 / 5 |
| infant_fever | 1 / 3 |
| pregnancy | 1 / 3 |
| self_harm | 3 / 4 |
| sepsis_meningitis | 3 / 3 |

"half my face has gone slack", "my words are coming out as nonsense" and "I
cannot lift my right arm at all" are the three things a stroke campaign teaches
people to say. None of them reaches the stroke category.

**Each miss sits a word or two from a phrase that fires today**, which is the
signature of a literal substring list rather than a vocabulary gap:

| reaches 911 | reaches nothing |
|---|---|
| "I took too many pills" → Poison Control | "I took the **whole bottle** of pills" |
| "curtain came over my eye" → EMERGENT | "curtain came **down over one** eye" |
| "threw up blood" → EMERGENT | "threw up something like **coffee grounds**" |
| "I have chest pain" → EMERGENT | "my chest **is being crushed**" |

**This is the same root cause as FINDING 1 of the 2026-09-19 run** — where 8
red-flag phrases lost EMERGENT when a contraction was written out in full.
That run measured brittleness along one axis (contractions); this one measures
it along another (paraphrase). Both are the literal-substring limitation this
repo already documents. The contraction finding remains open.

### What a miss actually costs

⛔ **Read FINDING 0 before this section.** A miss on its own defaults to
**URGENT** — "get this checked" — with `escalation_guidance` still attached.
What is absent is the category headline, the number, and the instruction to
ring it now: Poison Control for an overdose, 988 for self-harm. On its own,
read a miss as *the app said see someone soon where it should have said call
an ambulance.*

**But a miss is rarely on its own.** Put any of these in the same sentence as
an ordinary complaint — which is how people describe themselves — and 61% of
them come back SELF_CARE from the rule layer, because the minor phrase matches
positively and the missed red flag cannot take it back. That is FINDING 0, and
it is the real cost of this finding.

Verified end to end on the live deployment. Typing **"I took the whole bottle
of pills"** returns `status: needs_detail` and a questionnaire — *"Where in
your body do you feel it? How bad is it, from 1 to 10?"* — with `emergency:
null`.

### The mitigation that already exists, and its limit

`check_picker_coverage.py --strict` was re-run this session: **all 25 red-flag
phrases the `SymptomPicker` offers reach the category they should.** So a
person who *taps* "Chest pain" gets 911 reliably, and the tap path is not
affected by anything in this finding.

That is a real mitigation and it should be weighed. It is not a fix, for two
reasons. Most people describe a crisis in their own words rather than hunting a
list, and the picker offers 202 phrases of which only 52 are recognised by any
rule (25.7%) — the red flags are the well-covered corner of it. Someone typing
"my chest is being crushed" is not going to stop and scroll for "Chest pain".

### ⛔ Read the caveat as carefully as the number

These descriptions are **an engineer's idea of how a frightened person
writes**. Not transcripts, not validated, not clinician-reviewed — exactly the
standing of `corpus.py`'s gold labels, with the same limit. A real miss rate
needs real user text this project does not have and should not collect for
this purpose. What the sweep is good for is direction and magnitude, and a
category recognising none of six ordinary descriptions of itself is not a
borderline call.

### Why the 100% figures are not wrong

Both corpora measure agreement with documented intent, on phrasings somebody
thought to write down, and both say so plainly. This sweep is the other half.
The 59.5% rule coverage already hinted at it; this puts a number on what the
gap costs at the EMERGENT end.

Six of the 56 are additionally pinned in `corpus.py` as documented gaps, so
they appear `[OPEN]` in every `measure.py` run and are excluded from the
scores. A fix is a case moving out of that section.

## FIXED 2 — `/providers/search` never sorted by distance

A live search of 89109 returned **2.9, 1.6, (none), 2.5, 0.4, 1.9** miles in
that order: nearest clinic fifth, unplaceable one mid-list, whole page
alphabetical by name, which is the order NPPES supplies.

It could not have been otherwise — `distances_for` runs *after*
`search_providers` has fixed the order — while the code directly above the
response carried a ⛔ comment asserting "results stay sorted by distance only",
and `docs/appointments.md` said it twice.

Invisible because `ProviderSearchScreen` sorts again on arrival, so the one
client there is has always rendered correctly. That is why it was worth fixing:
distance-only ordering is the app's neutrality guarantee — what stops MedHelp
ranking clinics on any clinical or commercial ground — and a guarantee living
in one screen is one the next consumer silently does not get.

## FIXED 3 — the mutation checker reported success having run nothing

`node mobile/scripts/mutation_check.mjs --help`, or any typo of a group name,
matched zero mutations, printed an empty table, said **"Every mutation was
caught."** and exited 0.

The one script whose entire purpose is distrusting a green result failed its
own standard. In CI that is a green tick for work never done. Unknown groups
now exit 2 naming the valid ones, a run that mutated nothing is an error, and
the success line carries the count: "Every mutation was caught (8 of 8)".

The backend twin was already correct (exit 1). An earlier reading of 0 was
`tail`'s exit code through a pipe, not the script's.

## FIXED 4 — "when required" is PRN, and it was setting six alarms a day

`dose_schedule.AS_NEEDED` carried "as required", "when needed" and "if needed"
— three of the four combinations of {as, when} × {needed, required} — and
missed **"when required"**, which is exactly how a British label prints PRN.

So `TAKE 1 TABLET EVERY 4 HOURS WHEN REQUIRED` was read as a plain four-hourly
interval and proposed **six alarms a day, including 00:00 and 04:00** — the app
waking somebody at four in the morning to take an as-needed painkiller.
`every 6 hours if required` and `every 8 hours when necessary` did the same.

`docs/medication-reminders.md` calls this **the most important refusal** in the
module. It was live. Added: when required, if required, as/when/if necessary.

Found by the same method as FINDING 1 — running real-world inputs through a
phrase list rather than reading it — applied to the other phrase list in the
app. Two of the three lists tested this way have now yielded a defect.

⛔ One-directional and therefore safe: every addition can only make the function
refuse *more*, never schedule more. Verified no over-refusal — BID, QD, TID,
QID, bedtime and 12-hourly all still suggest as before.

### REPORTED, NOT FIXED: a finite course becomes a permanent alarm

`TAKE 1 TABLET DAILY FOR 7 DAYS THEN STOP` is suggested 09:00 daily with no
end. The daily part is right, so refusing it would lose a useful suggestion for
the case reminders help most with. What is missing is an end date, and
`medication_reminders` has no column for one — a feature and a migration, not a
phrase-list fix. Bounded today by `ReminderEditScreen` showing the printed
directions beside the proposed times.

## FIXED 5 — the emergency card's medication list could vanish silently

`docs/emergency-card.md` claimed the caps "keep the record inside Android's
SecureStore size limit (~2048 bytes)". False by a factor of seven: 25
medications with both fields at the 300-character cap serialise to **15,601
bytes**, and even ordinary names (40/20) reach 2,101 at 25 medications. At the
documented caps, **three** medications fit.

An oversized write is lost silently and `mirrorMedications` swallows the
failure because it must never break the medication screen — so the card showed
**no medications at all**, to a responder, with nothing indicating anything was
missing. Now budgeted by UTF-8 bytes; a long list degrades to a shorter one in
the person's own order.

⛔ **The existing test passed while proving nothing.** "caps the list, because
the record has to fit the platform keystore" used `"Synthetic 0"` / `"1 mg"` —
about 1 kB — so it exercised the count cap and never the byte limit it was
named for.

Separately pinned: **the card itself fits by 102 bytes** (1,946 of ~2048) and
only because it has exactly six fields. A seventh 300-character field would
take it over and lose the whole card, so the margin is now asserted by a test.

## FIXED 6 — the interpretation safety check let 14 of 20 unsafe answers through

`interpretation.py` is the newest code in the app (2026-09-19) and generates
model-written prose displayed beside a tier. `_is_safe` drops a named condition
at any tier and reassurance above SELF_CARE. Both patterns were written from
three example sentences each, and held little more than those.

**Naming a condition — the thing App Scope says the app may never do:**

    This looks like appendicitis.              KEPT
    These are classic signs of a migraine.     KEPT
    The likely cause is a sinus infection.     KEPT
    Sounds like flu to me.                     KEPT

The last walked through on a missing word: the pattern was
`sounds like (a|an|the)` and required an article.

**Reassurance printed beside an URGENT tier:**

    You should be fine.                        KEPT
    This will clear up on its own.             KEPT
    It can safely wait.                        KEPT

"It can safely wait" beside "get this seen soon" is exactly the property-4
violation the check exists to make impossible.

⛔ **Widening these lists is one-directional and therefore safe**, which is why
it was fixed rather than reported: a wider pattern can only drop *more* text,
and the module is built so that dropping is harmless — "every failure path
returns `None` and the screen renders exactly what it rendered before this
module existed". Over-dropping costs a nicety; under-dropping prints a
diagnosis. A test also pins that reassurance is still allowed **at** SELF_CARE,
where it is the honest answer.

As always, the check remains lexical. A model can still say something unsafe in
words no list holds, and no clinician has read this prompt.

  typing a password into a form is something this agent does not do, whoever

  `main`. So a change could be written but not measured, and this repo's own

  design.**
